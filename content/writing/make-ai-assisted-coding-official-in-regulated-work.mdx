In regulated work, developers need clear rules about which coding assistants they can use and what they can share with them. Even a routine request to explain an error can include data or internal details that belong in an approved environment.

I’d start with a short, practical policy covering approved tools, data handling, and who can answer questions. I’d use ordinary code review, tests, and dependency scanning for generated changes too. Reviewers should have enough context to know what a change is supposed to do and how it was checked.

Someone should also collect examples of where the tools helped or introduced problems. Those examples give the team something concrete to use in training and when revising its rules.
